Drove out to a dark sky site about forty minutes from the city last night with a thermos of tea and a blanket and spent three hours lying on the car bonnet watching the sky. The Milky Way was visible as an actual band across the sky, not just a vague brightening, which I haven't seen properly since I was a kid camping with my family. Light pollution does such extraordinary damage to the night sky that most people living in or near cities have never seen it as it actually looks. Was using an astronomy app to identify what I was looking at and spent about an hour working through the main constellations, connecting the classical stories to the star patterns, which made them stick in a way that just memorizing positions never has. Found the Andromeda Galaxy without help, which is the most distant thing visible to the naked eye at about two and a half million light years, and spent a long time with that fact. A thing you can see with no equipment other than eyes, two and a half million years old by the time it reaches you. Got cold around midnight and drove home with that particular mood that big sky thinking produces, somewhere between insignificance and peace. Going to get a beginner telescope before the winter sky arrives and the seeing gets better. The Pleiades cluster through even a small scope is supposed to be extraordinary.
