Sat down for another drawing session tonight and worked through the perspective exercises in the course I've been following. Two-point perspective is starting to click after weeks of it feeling completely arbitrary. The key insight for me was understanding that the horizon line is always at eye level regardless of what you're drawing, which sounds obvious when you say it but wasn't obvious to me at all when I was just trying to copy lines. I've been filling a sketchbook with boxes and architectural shapes and it's not glamorous but I can feel the spatial reasoning improving. My lines are still wobbly, I have a habit of drawing from my wrist rather than my shoulder which produces short tentative strokes instead of confident long ones. Been practicing ghosting, where you rehearse the motion in the air before committing to the paper, and it does help. The frustrating part about learning to draw as an adult is that your taste develops faster than your skill, so you can see clearly how far your work is from what you want it to be. Read something that described this as the gap, and the only way through it is volume. Going to aim for thirty minutes every evening and a longer session on weekend mornings. Already on my second sketchbook which feels like meaningful progress.
