Had my third surfing lesson today and managed to stand up and ride a wave properly for the first time, even if it only lasted about four seconds before I fell off the side. Those four seconds felt fantastic. The instructor has been very patient with me because I've been struggling with the pop-up, the movement from lying flat to standing, which needs to happen in one fluid motion and I keep breaking it into steps which kills the timing. Today something clicked and I stopped thinking about it as a sequence and just trusted my body to do it, and that's when it worked. The ocean is genuinely humbling in a way I didn't expect. You're dealing with something that has no interest in your success or failure and the variables are constantly changing: wave height, period, current, wind. You can't control any of it, you can only read it and respond. I've been told that most beginner surfers quit because the gap between understanding what to do and being able to do it is so wide and lasts so long. The fitness demand is also higher than it looks, paddling against broken whitewash is exhausting and my shoulders are completely done. Going to commit to weekly lessons through the summer and hopefully rent a board by myself by August. The sea here is cold enough to need a five-millimetre wetsuit but the breaks are gentle and forgiving, which is exactly what I need right now.
