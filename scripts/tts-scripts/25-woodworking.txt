Finished the carcass of the bookshelf I've been building over the past month and it's actually square, which feels like a genuine achievement given how badly my first attempt went. Had to learn the hard way that wood movement is real and you can't just glue up a large panel without accounting for it. The first shelf I built had a beautiful glue-up that cracked three weeks later because I'd attached it with screws that prevented it from expanding and contracting with humidity. This time I used figure-eight fasteners which allow movement and the whole thing feels much more solid. The dovetail joints on the top corners took three practice cuts before I got one I was happy with, hand-cut dovetails are genuinely tricky and I have enormous respect for people who do it quickly. The router table has been invaluable for the dados and rabbets, so much cleaner than trying to chisel them by hand. Still need to do the face frame, sand everything to two twenty grit, and apply the finish, probably going with a danish oil and a few coats of hard wax oil on top. The whole project has probably taken twenty times longer than buying a flat pack shelf, but there's something deeply satisfying about making something functional with your hands that I haven't found in much else.
