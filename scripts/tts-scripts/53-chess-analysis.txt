Spent an hour going over last night's online blitz games with the engine and it was the usual combination of useful and humbling. Blundered a winning position in two separate games by getting impatient in the endgame and pushing a passed pawn prematurely instead of activating my king first. The engine evaluation went from plus two to minus one in a single move both times, which is the kind of concrete feedback that makes chess analysis so effective. You can't argue with the number. My opening preparation is holding up reasonably well, I've been playing the London System consistently enough that I know the plans and ideas without having to think about them in the early moves. Where I keep losing time on the clock is in the early middlegame transitions when I'm trying to figure out the plan for the specific pawn structure that's resulted. Reading some material on pawn structures and the associated plans and it's clarifying some patterns I've been navigating by feel rather than understanding. My tactical vision has improved noticeably from doing a set of puzzles every morning, I'm seeing combinations two or three moves deeper than I was six months ago. The goal this year is to break the eleven hundred rating on lichess which requires eliminating the one-move blunders that keep costing me games I should win. Pattern recognition is the answer and pattern recognition requires volume.
