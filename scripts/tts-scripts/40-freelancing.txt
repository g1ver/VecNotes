Had a difficult week on the freelance side of things. A client who owes me two invoices from six weeks ago has gone quiet, which is a situation I've been in before and it never gets less stressful. The work was delivered, signed off, and the invoices were sent on time. I've followed up twice politely and once more directly, and the responses have been vague rather than hostile, which suggests cash flow problems on their end rather than a dispute about the work. Going to give it until end of week and then send a formal notice. The lesson I keep learning and not applying consistently enough is that contracts and payment terms need to be tight from the start, not just for protection but because clear terms prevent ambiguity that creates these situations. Thirty-day net payment terms are standard but I should be asking for a deposit upfront for larger projects, something I've been too cautious about for fear of seeming difficult to work with. The other thing this week taught me is how much cash buffer I actually need to feel comfortable with the income variability of freelance work. Three months of expenses feels like the right floor and I'm currently sitting at about six weeks which is too thin. Need to prioritize building that up before taking on any new commitments. Good work doesn't guarantee good clients and the business side of freelancing is a skill that needs as much attention as the craft itself.
