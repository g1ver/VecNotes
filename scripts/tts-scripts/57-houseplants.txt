Lost another plant this week to what I'm pretty sure is root rot, a monstera deliciosa I'd had for about eighteen months and was genuinely fond of. Checked the roots after pulling it from the pot and they were brown and mushy right to the base, which means the soil has been staying too wet for too long. I've been overwatering consistently because I water on a schedule rather than checking whether the soil actually needs it. Every source I've read says to wait until the top inch or two is dry before watering, which sounds simple, and I still keep forgetting. Now down to eight plants from eleven at my peak, which is a humbling attrition rate. The survivors are the ones that are genuinely hard to kill: a snake plant, two pothos in different propagation stages, a ZZ plant, and a bird of paradise that's been slowly, stubbornly putting out new leaves since last winter. Also have a monstera adansonii cutting I'm trying to propagate in water that's putting out roots at a promising rate. The lesson I keep taking too long to internalize is that most houseplants die from overcare rather than neglect. They evolved to survive periods of drought and they cope much better with too little water than too much. Switching to a soil moisture checker this week rather than relying on my own schedule-based guessing.
