Book club last night was on Meditations by Marcus Aurelius and it was the best discussion we've had in months. The book resists easy summary because it's not structured as an argument, it's a private journal, repetitive and unpolished, and the repetition is actually part of the point. Aurelius wasn't writing to teach philosophy, he was reminding himself of principles he believed but kept forgetting in practice. Several people found it comforting in a way they didn't expect. One member, who's been going through a difficult work situation, said it helped to read someone who had enormous power and responsibility and still struggled with anger, with wanting recognition, with doing the difficult thing when the easy thing was available. The universality of the struggle is the point. The distinction between things in our control and things outside our control came up a lot. The Stoic argument is simple: your judgments and intentions are in your control and almost nothing else is. Applying that frame to something like a performance review or a relationship difficulty changes how it sits. You can't control the outcome but you can control the quality of effort and character you bring. Someone pushed back on whether this collapses into passivity and we had a good argument about the difference between acceptance and resignation. Going to read the Ryan Holiday commentary edition before next month's session.
