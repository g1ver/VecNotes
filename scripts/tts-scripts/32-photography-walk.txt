Took the camera out for a few hours this morning with no particular destination or subject in mind, just walking and looking. The light at seven in the morning is genuinely different from any other time of day, softer and more directional, and I managed a few shots of the market being set up that I'm actually happy with. The vendors unloading boxes in the low sideways light with long shadows, that kind of thing. Street photography is different from any other kind because you're working entirely reactively. You can't set up or wait for the ideal moment in the same way, you just have to see what's there and respond quickly before it's gone. I missed at least four shots today because I was too slow to react or too hesitant to raise the camera. There's a social anxiety element to it that I haven't fully overcome, the discomfort of pointing a lens at strangers. Most people don't mind once they realize you're not there to mock them, but the moment before you know their reaction is uncomfortable. Shot about two hundred frames and probably twelve are worth keeping, which I've learned is a perfectly normal ratio and not a sign of failure. The practice is as much about learning to see as it is about the images themselves. Going to do this at least twice a week.
