Volunteered at the food bank for the first time today and it rearranged some of my assumptions about who uses them and why. I'd had a vague mental image of the people who come through, specific demographics, specific circumstances, and the reality was much more varied and ordinary than that image. Families, elderly people, working people who had a bad month, people who'd had a job last year and didn't have one now. The coordinator mentioned that demand has increased significantly over the past two years and that a meaningful proportion of their users are in employment, which suggests the problem isn't lack of work but that work isn't paying enough. The morning involved sorting donations, checking expiry dates, packing standardized boxes for different household sizes, and helping carry bags to cars. It was simple physical work and I found it genuinely grounding compared to the abstract work I do at a computer most of the week. There's something clarifying about a task with a clear output that directly helps a specific person. The team of regular volunteers was impressive, people who've been showing up every Saturday for years with no fanfare. Going to commit to twice a month rather than treating it as a one-off. The hour-and-a-half felt useful in a way that I want more of.
