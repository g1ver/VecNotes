Back from three nights in the hills and my baseline sense of what I actually need to feel comfortable has been helpfully reset. The first night was hard, cold, sleeping pad not quite warm enough, rain on the tent fly, woke up at three and couldn't get back to sleep. By the third night I slept straight through and woke up feeling better than I have in months. Something about the rhythm of camping, eating when you're hungry, sleeping when it gets dark, moving your body all day, brings you into alignment with something simpler. I cooked everything on a one-burner stove, ate the same three meals each day, and found it completely satisfying. The contrast with the choice paralysis of cooking at home, where you can make anything and somehow end up making nothing good, was striking. Walked about fifteen kilometres a day through moorland and some forest and didn't look at my phone for three days except to use it as a map. No social media, no news, no pings. The reentry this morning was a bit rough, I opened my email and immediately felt the familiar low-level stress of things waiting for responses. But I feel clearer about what I actually value and what I'm spending time on out of habit rather than intention. Trying to hold onto some of that simplicity rather than letting the noise rush back in completely.
