Went to a structured wine tasting at a bar in the city centre tonight, eight wines across four regions, and came away with a much more calibrated palate than I went in with. The host walked us through a proper tasting process: look at the color and clarity, swirl and smell before tasting, then actually taste and think about structure rather than just whether you like it. I've always been the kind of person who knows what they enjoy but couldn't articulate why, and tonight was the first time I felt like I had a vocabulary for it. Acidity, tannin, body, finish, they're not just wine snob terms, they're actual dimensions of what you're experiencing. The most interesting comparison was a Burgundy Pinot Noir next to a New Zealand Pinot Noir from Marlborough. Same grape, completely different wine. The Burgundy was earthy and restrained, almost savory, while the New Zealand one was fruit-forward, plummy, almost sweet by comparison. Climate and terroir doing real work there. My favorites of the evening were a Chablis for its minerality and a Côtes du Rhône that was just incredibly drinkable. Not going to pretend I retained everything but I left with a list of bottles to seek out and a bit more confidence about navigating a wine list without just defaulting to whatever's second cheapest.
