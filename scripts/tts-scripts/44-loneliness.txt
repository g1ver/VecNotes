Spent most of today alone and felt the particular weight of it that's different from just being by yourself. Loneliness isn't about the number of people around you, I've felt it in rooms full of people and I know that now, it's about whether you feel genuinely seen by someone. I've been in this city for two years and I have acquaintances, people I see at events and get along with fine, but very few people I'd call at eight on a Tuesday if something was wrong. Building deep friendships as an adult is genuinely hard in a way nobody really prepares you for. The structures that created friendships when you were younger, school, university, living in close proximity with people at a formative time, mostly disappear and you're left having to do something that feels almost artificially intentional. You can't just schedule depth. I've been trying to be more honest about wanting connection rather than performing self-sufficiency, which is the thing I default to when I'm uncomfortable. Reached out to someone I've been meaning to catch up with for months and made plans instead of leaving it as a vague intention. Small thing but it felt important. The research on loneliness as a health risk is sobering. It's not a personal failing or a sign of weakness. It's just something that needs tending to like anything else that matters.
