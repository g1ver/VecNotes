Went out early to the nature reserve at the edge of town this morning with borrowed binoculars and a field guide and spent two hours doing something I've never really done intentionally before, which is just watching birds. It sounds simple and it is, but there's a quality of attention it demands that's different from most other activities. You're scanning constantly, catching movement, identifying a shape in a bush, staying still so you don't flush whatever you're looking at. My mind, which is usually running commentary on everything, went quiet in a way that felt almost unfamiliar. Spotted about fourteen species, most of which I had to look up in the guide: a reed bunting that was incredibly close, a pair of great crested grebes doing their remarkable mirrored courtship dance on the far edge of the lake, and a kingfisher that sat on a branch for about forty seconds before diving. The kingfisher was the standout, that improbable electric blue and orange in a grey morning. I've seen them before but never had time to really look. The reserve warden I met on the path said the bitterns have been calling in the evenings which I want to come back for. There's a local birding group that does dawn walks twice a month and I'm going to join them, partly for the expertise in the group and partly because this kind of quiet shared attention seems like a good way to meet people.
