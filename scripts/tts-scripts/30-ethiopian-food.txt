Had dinner at an Ethiopian restaurant with a few friends last night and it completely reset my understanding of what a shared meal can feel like. Everything arrived on a single large injera, the spongy fermented flatbread that functions as both plate and utensil, with maybe eight different dishes piled on top of it: a lamb tibs, several lentil and chickpea wats with berbere spice, some greens cooked with garlic, and a pile of fresh salad. You tear off pieces of injera and use them to scoop everything. There's something fundamentally communal about eating that way, you're all reaching toward the same surface and navigating around each other. The berbere spice blend was the flavor I want to understand better, warm and complex with what I think are cardamom, cloves, fenugreek, and chili alongside more familiar spices. The lamb had a depth from long slow cooking that was extraordinary. I had no idea Ethiopian cuisine was this sophisticated and I'm annoyed it took me this long to try it properly, rather than just the one mediocre place I went to years ago. Already looking at recipes because I want to attempt a misir wat, the red lentil dish, at home. The injera I'll probably just buy from the restaurant rather than try to make my own teff starter.
