Spent the afternoon going deep on window functions in SQL and finally feel like I understand them properly after months of cargo-culting examples I found online. The mental model that made them click for me is thinking of them as a calculation that happens after the GROUP BY but before the final output, and unlike aggregate functions they don't collapse rows, they just add a column to each existing row with the calculated value. Worked through a bunch of exercises involving running totals, rank, and lag, which are the ones that come up constantly in analytics work. The lag function in particular is something I've been writing clunky subqueries to replicate for years without realizing there was a cleaner way. Also got more comfortable with the OVER clause and partition by, understanding that partition by is essentially like GROUP BY but for just that window calculation. The thing about SQL is that the basics are deceptively simple and you can do a lot with SELECT, FROM, WHERE, and GROUP BY, but there's a whole second tier of capability in window functions, CTEs, and recursive queries that most people never reach because the basics are good enough for most tasks. Going to start deliberately using window functions in my actual work queries to cement the knowledge before it fades.
