Finished building the mechanical keyboard last night and it's genuinely one of the more satisfying things I've made. The whole process took about two weekends spread across a month: sourcing the parts, waiting on shipping from three different countries, lubing sixty-seven switches by hand which is as tedious as it sounds, soldering them to the PCB, assembling everything, and flashing the firmware. The result is a board that sounds and feels completely different from anything you can buy off the shelf. The switches are Boba U4Ts, tactile and relatively quiet, and with the lubing they have this buttery bump that's hard to describe but immediately noticeable when you type on it. Added PE foam inside the case for sound dampening which gave it a deeper thock instead of the ping that unmodified keyboards produce. The layout is sixty-five percent which means no numpad, no function row, and some keys handled through layers, which I was nervous about but have adapted to faster than expected. The total cost came to about two hundred and eighty pounds which is obviously irrational compared to a thirty-pound keyboard that does the same job. But the things you make yourself have a different relationship with you than things you buy, and I'll be using this for years. Already thinking about what the next build would be and I've only had this one for three days.
