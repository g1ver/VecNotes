Had one of those rare genuinely productive mornings where everything else fell away and I just worked for three and a half hours straight without checking my phone or opening a new tab. Was finishing a complex data pipeline that had been giving me grief for days and something about the quiet of the early morning just let me actually think clearly about it. Turned off notifications at seven, made a cup of coffee, and told myself I wasn't touching anything else until noon. The first thirty minutes were still a little scattered, my brain kept wanting to drift toward email and messages, but after that threshold something clicked and I was fully in it. The code came together in a way that felt almost effortless compared to the fragmented sessions I'd been having all week. I've read a lot about deep work as a concept but experiencing the difference between that kind of focused session and my usual interrupted workday is striking every time. The problem isn't knowing that focus matters, it's building the environmental conditions to make it possible consistently. Going to try blocking three mornings a week for this kind of no-meeting, no-notifications work and see if I can make it a real habit rather than a happy accident.
