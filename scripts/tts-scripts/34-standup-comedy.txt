Did my third open mic night last week and it went better than the first two, which is a low bar, but still. Got some genuine laughs on two of the bits I'd been working on, including the one about trying to explain my job to my parents that I'd been convinced wasn't landing. Turns out it was my delivery that was killing it rather than the writing. I'd been rushing through the setup because I was nervous, not giving the audience time to follow the logic before hitting the punchline. Slowing down and trusting the material made a real difference. The bit I thought was my strongest died completely, which is always instructive. It's too inside, too reliant on the audience sharing a specific experience, and when they don't have that reference point the whole thing collapses. Comedy writing is ruthlessly empirical in a way I find oddly satisfying. Something either works in the room or it doesn't, and there's no arguing with that result. Been going to a weekly comedy workshop which is helping me understand structure better: setup, misdirect, punchline, tag. The theory isn't that complicated but applying it consistently is a skill that takes reps. Going to try to do at least two mics a month and keep writing ten minutes a day minimum, even if most of it is bad. Especially if most of it is bad.
