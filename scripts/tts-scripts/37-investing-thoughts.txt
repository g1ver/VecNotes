Spent some time reading about portfolio allocation this week after getting anxious watching the market dip over the past few weeks. The reading was a useful corrective because it reminded me of everything I already know intellectually but forget emotionally when things are going down. The expected return of stocks over any given twenty-year period has historically been positive. Trying to time the market consistently is nearly impossible and the cost of being wrong is missing the best days of recovery. My allocation is already pretty boring on purpose: eighty percent global index funds, fifteen percent bonds, five percent cash, which is what I set up two years ago and haven't touched since. The urge to do something, to react, is strong when things are moving, but the evidence pretty clearly shows that doing less is better for most people. What I'm less clear on is whether I should be rebalancing quarterly or letting it drift further before I intervene. Read a few things suggesting that less frequent rebalancing, maybe annually, has similar outcomes with lower tax drag and transaction costs. Going to set a calendar reminder to review properly in January and otherwise try to stop checking the portfolio balance more than once a month. The anxiety of watching daily fluctuations is genuinely counterproductive and I know better.
