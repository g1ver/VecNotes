Went to an industry networking event last night and spent the first forty minutes hovering near the food table trying to convince myself to actually talk to someone. Classic social anxiety behavior that I recognize in myself but find hard to interrupt in the moment. Eventually got into a conversation that went well, one person became two, and by the end of the evening I was genuinely enjoying myself, which is always how these things go and yet never makes the beginning easier. The thing that eventually got me moving was a tactic I read about where instead of trying to join a conversation, you look for someone else who's also standing alone, because they're just as relieved to have someone approach them as you would be. Found someone looking at the schedule board and started there. We ended up talking for an hour and they introduced me to three other people. The anxiety beforehand was so much worse than the reality, which is the pattern with social anxiety generally. The threat the brain constructs is almost always larger than what actually happens. I know this intellectually and still fell for it. Going to try attending at least one event a month to get more comfortable with the discomfort rather than avoiding it entirely. The connections I've made at these things have been genuinely valuable professionally and the cost of not going is higher than it feels in the moment.
