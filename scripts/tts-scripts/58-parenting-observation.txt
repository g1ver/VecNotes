Had my niece stay for the weekend and came away with a renewed appreciation for how much patience parenting demands, and I only had her for forty-eight hours with no other responsibilities competing for my attention. She's four and a half and at a stage where everything is a question or a negotiation, which is charming in thirty-minute bursts and genuinely exhausting over a full day. The thing I noticed watching her is how completely present she is in whatever she's doing. No part of her is somewhere else. She spent forty-five minutes this morning making a small village out of sticks and leaves in the garden and was entirely absorbed by it in a way I haven't been in anything for months. Adults talk a lot about being present and children just are. The negotiations around bedtime and food are legendary and I now understand why parents of young children talk about it the way they do. I tried to pick battles sensibly, let the small things go and held firm on the actually important things like sleep and not eating biscuits for dinner. She tested every boundary cheerfully and without malice and was asleep within minutes of her head touching the pillow. Dropped her home this morning and my flat felt very quiet. Not quiet in a bad way, but the particular quality of quiet after something lively has been and gone.
