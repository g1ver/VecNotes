Hit a real inflection point with German this week. After about eight months of fairly consistent study I had my first conversation where I wasn't consciously translating every sentence in my head before speaking. It was brief, just ordering food and making small talk with the owner of a German bakery near my office, but the words were just there, I wasn't reaching for them. It's a strange sensation after so long of the language feeling like a puzzle I'm solving in real time. The grammar still trips me up, particularly the case system, which doesn't exist in English and forces you to think about the grammatical role of every noun before you can get the article right. Der, die, das, and then all the inflected forms in accusative and dative, it's a lot of overhead. I've been using a mix of Anki for vocabulary, a weekly conversation partner session over video call, and watching German TV with subtitles, which has helped with listening comprehension more than anything else. The goal is to reach B2 by the time I visit Berlin in October, which feels achievable if I maintain the current pace. The lesson from these eight months is that consistency matters more than intensity, thirty minutes every day has done more than occasional three-hour sessions ever did.
