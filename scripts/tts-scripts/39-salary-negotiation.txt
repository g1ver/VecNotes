Had the salary negotiation conversation today and it went better than I expected, largely because I'd done the preparation properly this time instead of just hoping they'd offer something fair. Came in with three data points from industry salary surveys, a clear articulation of what I'd delivered in the past year, and a specific number rather than a range. Everything I'd read said anchoring high with a specific number is more effective than giving a range because ranges just tell the other person which end to offer. They came back below my number but above their initial offer, which is exactly the dynamic I expected. Accepted the counter because it was within the range I'd actually decided was acceptable, not because I'd run out of things to say. The thing I noticed is that the discomfort of the conversation is mostly anticipatory, once you're in it and staying calm and fact-based it's just a negotiation, not a confrontation. My manager seemed to respect the directness rather than being put off by it, which tracks with the advice that companies expect people to negotiate and factor in some buffer for that. Should have done this more confidently at my last two jobs instead of accepting the first offer each time. The compounding effect of underpaying yourself for years is significant. Going to revisit compensation every year going forward regardless of whether I feel like the moment is right.
