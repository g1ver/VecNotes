Finally got a decent sourdough loaf out of the oven today after about six failed attempts over the past two months. The crust has that proper blistered sheen and the crumb is open and chewy rather than the dense brick I kept producing before. The thing that made the difference was a combination of two changes: a longer cold ferment in the fridge overnight, and actually scoring the dough deeply enough before baking. I'd been scoring too timidly and the loaf was bursting at the sides instead of opening along the score. Also started baking inside a preheated Dutch oven which traps steam and dramatically improves crust development. The starter, which I named Edgar and have been keeping alive for three months, seems to be in good health, doubling reliably within four to six hours of feeding. Temperature is apparently everything with sourdough and I've been much more careful about keeping the dough in a consistent environment during bulk fermentation, around twenty-four degrees, rather than just leaving it on the counter wherever. Still working on shaping, my tension isn't quite right and the loaves spread a bit more than ideal during final proof. Going to watch more videos on pre-shaping and practice the technique with a cheap dough before doing it with a full batch. But today's loaf I'd actually serve to someone, which is a first.
