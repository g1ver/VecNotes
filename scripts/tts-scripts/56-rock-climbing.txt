Made it up a V4 boulder problem today that I've been projecting for three weeks and the moment of topping out felt completely disproportionate to what it objectively is, which is climbing a twelve-foot wall in a gym. The problem has a dynamic move in the middle, a jump from a sloper to a crimp rail, that requires committing fully and trusting your hands to land right. I'd been trying to do it statically, reaching instead of jumping, and it kept failing because the reach puts you in the wrong body position at the moment of contact. Once I committed to the dynamic movement and generated proper hip momentum the hands landed well and the rest of the route followed naturally. Technique over strength is the lesson climbing keeps teaching me and I keep having to relearn it. Watched some of the stronger climbers in the gym today and the difference isn't that they're pulling harder, it's that their footwork is precise and deliberate in a way mine isn't yet, which lets them generate upward movement from their legs rather than their arms. My fingers are getting stronger, the skin is toughening up, and I can feel the improved grip endurance compared to when I started three months ago. Going to focus specifically on slab climbing for the next few sessions because it forces technical footwork in a way that steep overhanging routes don't, and my feet are definitely my weak point.
