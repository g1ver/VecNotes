Got a bit obsessive about pour-over brewing this week after reading a long piece about extraction. The basic idea I kept circling back to is that coffee extraction is about dissolving the right compounds in the right order: acids first, then sweetness, then bitter and astringent compounds last. Under-extracted coffee is sour and thin, over-extracted is bitter and harsh, and the goal is to hit the sweet zone in the middle. I have a decent grinder but I'd been using a fairly coarse grind to make the process forgiving, which was probably leaving a lot of sweetness behind. Tried grinding finer this morning and slowing down the pour to extend the brew time and the difference in the cup was meaningful. More body, more sweetness, less of that sharp acidic bite I'd written off as just the character of this bean. Also paid more attention to water temperature, about ninety-two degrees rather than boiling, which is apparently important for lighter roasts where high heat accentuates sourness. The Kenyan natural process bean I've been working through is genuinely excellent when brewed right, blueberry and dark chocolate notes that I'd been muting with a lazy extraction. Going to experiment with different grind sizes for the rest of the bag and take notes on what produces the best cup.
