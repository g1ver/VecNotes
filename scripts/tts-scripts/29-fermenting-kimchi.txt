Made my first batch of kimchi last weekend and opened the jar today after five days of fermentation at room temperature. The smell is pungent in exactly the right way, funky and sour and garlicky, and the flavor has developed this complex depth that store-bought kimchi never quite has. The process was more involved than I expected, salting the cabbage for several hours to draw out moisture and then rinsing and drying it before mixing with the paste. The paste itself is gochugaru, fish sauce, garlic, ginger, and a small amount of rice flour paste to help it cling to the vegetables. I made it fairly traditional with napa cabbage and daikon radish, though next batch I want to try a version with Asian chives. The fermentation science is fascinating: naturally occurring lactobacillus bacteria on the vegetables convert sugars to lactic acid, which is what produces the sour flavor and also preserves the kimchi. The salt concentration matters a lot, too little and you risk unwanted bacteria, too much and you inhibit fermentation entirely. Moved the jar to the fridge now to slow fermentation and develop flavor over weeks. It pairs incredibly well with the fried rice I made tonight. Going to make a much bigger batch next time because this is going to disappear fast.
