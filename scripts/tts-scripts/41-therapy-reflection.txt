Left therapy today with a lot to sit with. We got into the pattern my therapist has been gently pointing at for a few sessions now, which is that I tend to manage other people's emotional states as a way of avoiding my own discomfort. When someone is upset I go into problem-solving mode immediately, offering solutions or reframing their situation, and she asked me today whether I do that for them or for myself. I didn't have a good answer. It's probably both, but I think more of it is about my discomfort with other people's distress than actual helpfulness. She used the phrase emotional labor as avoidance, which I'm still unpacking. The connection to my childhood she drew is one I've heard before but it landed differently today, maybe because I wasn't defending against it as hard. I grew up in a house where managing someone else's mood was a survival skill and I never unlearned it. In relationships now it means I can be supportive in a way that looks caring but is actually distancing, always the helper, never the one who needs help. The homework is to notice when I reach for problem-solving in a conversation and pause long enough to ask whether the other person actually wants a solution or just wants to be heard. Simple idea, probably harder to do than it sounds. Going back in two weeks.
